Exploring Australia's National Parks

Explore Australia's national parks through their geographic distribution, protected land area, history and visitor patterns. Discover which states protect the greatest areas of national park land, which parks attract the most visitors, and how park visitation has changed over time.

1. Which States Have the Most Land Protected as National Parks?

National parks protect important natural landscapes throughout Australia, but the total area protected as national parks differs considerably between states and territories. This choropleth map compares the total area of national park land in each Australian state and territory using CAPAD 2024 data. Each state is coloured according to the combined area of its national parks, measured in hectares. Darker shades of green indicate greater total national park area, while lighter shades represent smaller areas. Hover over a state to view its total national park area in both hectares and square kilometres.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) ; Australian states GeoJSON . Values represent the combined mapped area of non-overlapping national park records in CAPAD 2024. The map compares absolute protected area rather than the percentage of each state protected. Other protected-area categories are excluded.

2. Where Are Australia's National Parks Concentrated?

National parks are distributed across Australia, from coastal regions to remote inland landscapes. Each green dot represents a national park location recorded in CAPAD 2024. Use the state filter to explore park locations within different states and territories. Hover over individual dots to see the park name, state and recorded land area.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) ; Australian states GeoJSON . Dots represent park locations rather than exact geographic boundaries.

3. Where Are NSW's Most Visited National Parks?

Some national parks receive significantly more visits than others. This map compares selected NSW national parks using proportional circles positioned at their geographic locations. Larger circles indicate greater estimated domestic visitation during 2024–25. Hover over each circle to view its park name and estimated number of domestic visits.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) ; NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25 . Selected parks only. Circle area represents estimated domestic visits.

4. Which National Parks Protect the Most Land?

Australia's national parks vary dramatically in size. While some protect relatively small areas, others cover vast landscapes across the country. This treemap compares the 40 largest national parks recorded in CAPAD 2024. Each rectangle represents a park, with its area proportional to the amount of protected land. Colours distinguish Australian states and territories. Hover over a rectangle to explore the park's name, state and area in hectares and square kilometres.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) . The 40 largest parks by mapped non-overlapping national park area.

5. When Were Australia's National Parks First Protected?

Australia's national park network has developed over many decades. This heatmap groups currently listed national parks according to their original protected-area gazettal dates. Each column represents a decade, while each row represents a state or territory. Darker green cells indicate a greater number of parks first protected during that period. Hover over a cell to view the number of parks.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) . Original protection dates may precede national park designation.

6. How Does National Park Size Differ Between States?

National parks vary considerably in size across Australia, from small protected areas to vast landscapes covering thousands of square kilometres. This box-and-whisker plot compares the distribution of national park sizes across states and territories. Boxes show the middle half of the park-size distribution, while whiskers and outliers demonstrate variation. Orange diamonds identify the largest national park in each state. The horizontal axis uses a logarithmic scale to accommodate large differences in size.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) . Area is measured in square kilometres.

7. How Does Age Composition Vary by Visitor Origin?

Adult and child visits to NSW parks and reserves vary depending on visitor origin. This mosaic chart compares domestic visits from Sydney, Regional NSW and interstate visitors. Each column's width represents its share of total domestic visits, while coloured sections represent the proportions of visits made by adults and children. Interstate visits have the highest child proportion at approximately 29.3%, compared with 17.7% for Sydney and 14.9% for Regional NSW. Hover over rectangles for visit estimates and percentages.

Source: NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25 . Figures represent domestic visits, not unique visitors.

8. What Distinguishes NSW's Most Visited National Parks?

Are NSW's most frequently visited national parks also the largest or longest protected? This parallel coordinates plot compares selected NSW parks across three characteristics: park area, years since initial protection, and annual domestic visits. Each line represents one park, connecting its measurements across the three dimensions. Values are normalised between 0 and 1 to enable comparisons between different units. Park area is logarithmically transformed before normalisation. Use the dropdown menu to highlight individual national parks and explore their characteristics.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) ; NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25 . Includes matched parks with complete records. Years protected are calculated relative to 2024.

9. What Are NSW's 15 Most Visited Parks and Reserves?

NSW's most popular parks and reserves attract millions of domestic visits annually. This horizontal bar chart ranks the 15 most visited NPWS-managed parks and reserves during 2024–25. Longer bars represent more estimated annual domestic visits, and the top three are highlighted in dark green. Blue Mountains National Park leads the ranking with approximately 7.83 million visits. Hover over each bar to explore its ranking and estimated visitation.

Source: NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25 . The ranking includes national parks and other NPWS-managed reserves. Values represent visits rather than unique visitors.

10. How Has NSW Park Visitation Changed Since 2008?

Domestic visits to NSW NPWS-managed parks and reserves have increased substantially since 2008. This line chart tracks estimated annual domestic visits across nine survey periods from 2008 through the 2024–25 financial year. Visits increased from approximately 37.93 million in 2008 to 65.59 million in FY2024–25. The chart also highlights the decline between 2018 and 2020, followed by recovery in later survey periods. Each point represents an annual visit estimate for a surveyed period rather than an observation for every intervening year. Hover over the points to compare total, adult and child visits.

Source: NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey FY2024–25 . Earlier estimates cover calendar years, while FY2024–25 covers a financial year and uses updated survey methodology. Historical comparisons require caution.

11. Do Larger National Parks Attract More Visitors?

Does a larger protected area mean more visitors? This scatterplot compares land area with estimated domestic visitation for matched NSW national parks. Each circle represents one national park. Parks further to the right cover more land, while parks higher on the chart attract more estimated domestic visits. The horizontal axis uses a logarithmic scale because park sizes vary substantially. Click a circle to highlight an individual park and double-click to reset the selection. Hover over circles to view park names, areas in square kilometres and annual visit estimates.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) ; NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25 . Includes matching NSW national parks with positive land area and visitation estimates. The chart shows association, not causation.
