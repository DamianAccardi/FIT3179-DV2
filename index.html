Exploring Australia's National Parks

Explore where Australia's national parks are located, which parks attract the most visitors, how park sizes and protection dates vary, and how visitation has changed over time and relates to protected land area.

1. Where Do Australians Visit NSW National Parks?

National parks across New South Wales attract millions of domestic visits each year. This choropleth highlights selected parks, with darker shades of green representing higher estimated domestic visitation in 2024–25. Hover over a park to explore its visitation and land area.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW); NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25. Selected parks only.

2. Where Are Australia's National Parks Concentrated?

National parks are distributed across Australia, from coastal regions to remote inland landscapes. Each dot represents a park location recorded by CAPAD 2024. Use the state filter to explore different regions, and hover over dots for park names, state and area.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW); Australian states GeoJSON. Dots represent park locations, not exact boundaries.

3. Where Are NSW's Most Visited National Parks?

This map compares selected NSW national parks using circles positioned at their geographic locations. Larger circles indicate more estimated domestic visits in 2024–25. Hover over a circle to explore its park name and visit estimate.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW); NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25. Selected parks only.

4. Which National Parks Protect the Most Land?

Australia's national parks vary dramatically in size. This treemap compares the 40 largest national parks recorded in CAPAD 2024. Each rectangle represents a park, with rectangle area proportional to protected land area and colour representing its state or territory. Hover to explore park names and area.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW). The 40 largest parks by mapped non-overlapping protected area.

5. When Were Australia's National Parks First Protected?

This heatmap groups the original protected-area gazettal dates of currently listed national parks by state and decade. Darker cells represent decades in which more of those parks were first protected. Hover over a cell to examine the number of parks.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW). Original protection may precede national park designation.

6. How Does National Park Size Differ Between States?

This box-and-whisker plot compares the distribution of park sizes across Australian states and territories. Boxes show the middle half of park sizes, while whiskers and outliers reveal variation. Orange diamonds identify the largest park in each state. Park area is displayed on a logarithmic horizontal axis.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW). Area is measured in square kilometres.

7. How Does Age Composition Vary by Visitor Origin?

This mosaic chart compares adult and child visits to NSW NPWS-managed parks and reserves from Sydney, Regional NSW and interstate. The width of a column represents its share of domestic visits, while the coloured sections show adult and child proportions. Interstate visits have the highest child proportion, at approximately 29.3%.

Source: NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25. Domestic visits, rather than unique visitors.

8. What Distinguishes NSW's Most Visited National Parks?

This parallel coordinates plot compares selected NSW parks across area, years since first protection and annual domestic visits. Each line represents one matched park. The measures are normalised to a common scale and the park-area dimension is logarithmically transformed. Use the park dropdown to highlight individual lines.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW); NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25. Only parks matched across datasets with complete records are shown; years protected are relative to 2024.

9. What Are NSW's 15 Most Visited Parks and Reserves?

This ranked bar chart shows the 15 NPWS-managed parks and reserves receiving the most estimated domestic visits during 2024–25. Longer bars indicate more visits, and the top three are highlighted in dark green. Blue Mountains National Park leads the ranking with about 7.83 million visits.

Source: NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25. Figures represent visits, not unique visitors. Includes national parks and other managed reserves.

10. How Has NSW Park Visitation Changed Since 2008?

Domestic visits to NSW NPWS-managed parks and reserves increased from 37.93 million in 2008 to a record 65.59 million in FY2024–25. The trend also shows a fall between 2018 and 2020, during the period affected by bushfires and COVID-19 restrictions, before recovering. Each point is an annual visit estimate for a survey year, not an observation for every intervening year. Hover over a point to see total, adult and child visits.

Source: NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey FY2024–25, page 8. The earlier estimates cover calendar years, while FY2024–25 covers a financial year and uses an updated survey methodology. Comparisons require caution.

11. Do Larger National Parks Attract More Visitors?

Does a larger protected area mean more visitors? This scatterplot compares the land area of NSW national parks with estimated annual domestic visits in 2024–25. Each circle represents one park matched between CAPAD 2024 and the NSW Park Visitor Survey. Parks further to the right cover more land, while parks higher on the chart receive more visits.

The horizontal axis uses a logarithmic scale because park areas vary greatly. Click a circle to highlight a park and fade the others; double-click to clear the selection. Hover over circles to see the park name, area in square kilometres and visit estimates, with numerical values shown to two decimal places. The plot shows association, not causation.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW); NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25. Only matching NSW national parks with positive area and estimated domestic visits are shown. Visits are not unique visitors.
