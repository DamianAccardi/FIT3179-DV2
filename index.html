Exploring Australia's National Parks

Discover where Australia's national parks are located, how they are distributed across the country, which parks attract the most visitors, and how protected land has developed over time.

1. Where Do Australians Visit NSW National Parks?

National parks across New South Wales attract millions of domestic visits each year. This map highlights selected national parks, with darker shades of green representing higher estimated annual domestic visits during 2024–25. Hover over a park to explore its visitor numbers and land area.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) ; NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25 . Selected parks only.

2. Where Are Australia's National Parks Concentrated?

Australia's national parks are distributed across all states and territories, from coastal regions to remote inland landscapes. Each green dot represents a national park location recorded in the Collaborative Australian Protected Areas Database (CAPAD) 2024. Use the state filter to explore national park locations within different Australian states and territories. Hover over individual dots to see the park name, state and recorded land area.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) . State boundaries: australian-states GeoJSON . Dots indicate representative park locations, not geographic boundaries.

3. Where Are NSW's Most Visited National Parks?

Some national parks receive significantly more visits than others. This map compares selected NSW national parks using proportional circles, where larger circles represent higher estimated domestic visits in 2024–25. The position of each circle represents the national park's geographic location. Hover over a circle to explore its park name and estimated annual domestic visits.

Sources: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) ; NSW NPWS Park Visitor Survey 2024–25 . Circle area represents estimated domestic visits. Selected parks only.

4. Which National Parks Protect the Most Land?

Australia's national parks vary dramatically in size. While some protect relatively small areas, others cover vast landscapes across the country. This treemap compares the 40 largest national parks in Australia using data from CAPAD 2024. Each rectangle represents a national park. Larger rectangles indicate a greater area of protected land, while different colours represent Australian states and territories. Hover over a rectangle to explore the national park's name, state and protected land area in hectares and square kilometres.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) . The 40 largest national parks by mapped non-overlapping protected area.

5. When Were Australia's National Parks First Protected?

Australia's network of national parks and protected natural areas has developed over many decades. This heatmap compares when currently listed national parks were first recorded as protected areas across Australian states and territories. Each column represents a decade, while each row represents a state or territory. Darker green cells indicate a greater number of parks first protected during that period. Hover over a cell to explore the exact number of parks and compare historical trends between states.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) . Includes currently listed national parks with usable original gazettal dates. Initial protection may precede national park designation.

6. How Does National Park Size Differ Between States?

National parks vary considerably in size across Australia, from small protected areas to vast natural landscapes covering thousands of square kilometres. This box-and-whisker plot compares national park sizes across Australian states and territories. The middle line of each box represents the median park size, while the box covers the middle 50% of national parks. The whiskers show the range of typical park sizes, while individual points beyond the whiskers indicate unusually small or large parks. Orange diamonds highlight the largest national park in each state. Hover over these diamonds to identify the park and view its area. The horizontal axis uses a logarithmic scale to accommodate the large differences in park sizes. Each major step represents a tenfold increase.

Source: CAPAD 2024 (DCCEEW) . Park sizes are calculated from mapped non-overlapping protected areas. Area values are displayed in square kilometres.
